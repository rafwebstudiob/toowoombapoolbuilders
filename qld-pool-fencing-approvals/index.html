QLD pool fencing and approvals in Toowoomba

In short: A new pool in Queensland needs building approval, a compliant safety barrier, certification before it is filled to 300 mm or more, and registration with the QBCC.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

This page summarises official Queensland rules so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice and doesn't replace a building certifier or a QBCC-licensed pool safety inspector. Follow the links and check with the certifier or your council if you're unsure.

The short answer

A new swimming pool in Queensland needs building development approval and must comply with the pool safety laws. The pool and its barrier must be inspected and certified before the pool is filled to a depth of 300 mm or more. Regulated pools must also be registered with the Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC). We don't arrange approvals; a local builder and a private building certifier usually handle that.

Building approval

The QBCC says that before building a pool you must get building approval from your local council or a private certifier, and that in most cases the pool builder arranges it. The certifier will tell you if you need a structural report on a difficult site, or other approvals such as building over a sewer.

Sources: QBCC, Build a pool; qld.gov.au, Pool fencing and safety laws.

Local council: Toowoomba

Your local council is Toowoomba Regional Council. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a pool. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Filling the pool and temporary fencing

The certifier who approved the pool must inspect and certify the fence before the pool is filled to a depth of 300 mm or more. A temporary fence can be used for up to three months only if the same certifier has inspected and approved it. A permanent compliant fence is needed before the final certificate.

The pool safety standard

The Queensland standard for pool barriers is Queensland Development Code Mandatory Part 3.4 (QDC MP 3.4). The QBCC's pool fences page says fences must be at least 1,200 mm high, with a gap of no more than 100 mm at the bottom, built on stable ground, and with a non-climbable zone of at least 900 mm between rails. The standard also covers gates, latches and direct access from a building.

Registering the pool and pool safety certificates

All regulated pools must be registered on the QBCC pool register. A Form 23 pool safety certificate, issued only by a QBCC-licensed pool safety inspector, is needed when you sell, buy or lease a property with a regulated pool. It lasts one year for shared pools and two years for non-shared pools.

Source: QBCC, Inspect and certify a pool.

Contracts, deposits and licences

In Queensland, the QBCC says domestic building work over $3,300 needs a written contract, and the contractor must arrange Queensland Home Warranty Scheme cover. The deposit before work starts can be no more than 10% for contracts from $3,301 to $19,999, or 5% for contracts of $20,000 or more. Contracts over $3,300 also come with a cooling-off period (generally five business days). Check the contractor's QBCC licence before you sign.

Sources: QBCC, Domestic building contracts; QBCC, Deposits and progress payments; QBCC, Cooling-off period.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources or a building certifier before relying on this summary.
